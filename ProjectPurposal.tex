\section*{Task Overview and Significance}

Our task is to recognize defensive coverages before the snap. This is challenging because defenses often try to disguise their coverage by showing one formation or alignment before the snap and executing another after the snap. This makes it difficult for an offensive player or coordinator to determine the defense's true coverage based only on its pre-snap alignment.

This matters because accurately identifying defensive coverage before the play begins could help offensive coordinators and quarterbacks make better pre-snap decisions, such as adjusting the play, route combinations, or blocking scheme.

This project is non-trivial because the model must make its prediction using only pre-snap information, while accounting for defensive disguises, player positioning, and different formations. The novel aspect of our project is developing a machine learning approach specifically focused on predicting the coverage before the snap, rather than using information from the play after it has already developed. We will also investigate which pre-snap features are most useful for distinguishing between different defensive coverages.

\section*{Data Sources and Collection Plan}
We downloaded the \href{https://github.com/ThompsonJamesBliss/nfl-big-data-bowl-regional-event-data}{Big Data Bowl regional-event dataset}: 122 games from weeks 1--8 of the 2021 season. Its documentation identifies NFL Next Gen Stats as the tracking provider and Pro Football Focus (PFF) as the scouting and coverage-label provider. \href{https://edge-operations.nfl.com/game-operations-logistics/technology/performance-tracking-data-next-gen-stats}{Next Gen Stats} collects player movement through stadium receivers and RFID tags at 10 samples per second. The \href{https://operations.nfl.com/programs-initiatives/innovation/big-data-bowl}{NFL} organizes the release; detailed PFF annotation procedures are not documented.

We obtained CSVs directly from GitHub after the original \href{https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/nfl-big-data-bowl-2025/data}{Kaggle 2025 dataset} was removed. No scraping or API polling is needed. Raw data stays in the ignored \texttt{data/regional-2021/} directory; a manifest records the source revision and file hashes. Data-use and redistribution terms remain to be verified.

The release contains game, play, player, PFF scouting, and per-game tracking tables. We join plays and tracking on \texttt{gameId}/\texttt{playId}, and player metadata on \texttt{nflId}. Available metadata includes week, teams, player positions, timestamps, direction, and snap events. Inputs will combine player coordinates, speed and acceleration with down, yards to go, clock, and pre-snap scores. The existing \texttt{pff\_passCoverage} labels provide 12 observed classes; no new annotation is required.

We will retain the last complete frame strictly before each play's \texttt{ball\_snap}, with 11 distinct players per team and valid coordinates, then normalize direction and positions relative to the line of scrimmage. One snapshot per play keeps the input small while preserving alignment. We exclude coverage annotations, post-snap frames, descriptions, and outcomes from predictors. Games stay together across splits; preprocessing is fitted on training data only.

\textbf{Dataset size:} There are 8,557 plays and 8,314,178 tracking rows. The initial audit retained 8,522 labeled plays with complete pre-snap snapshots and excluded 35 that failed these checks. Plays, rather than frames, count as instances; further quality checks may reduce this total.
